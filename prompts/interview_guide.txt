Generate a structured interview guide in clean markdown. Follow Torres' interview principles throughout: start with the recent past, ask about specific experiences (not opinions), and never ask "would you" or "do you think" questions.

Use the following structure:

**Opening**
Write one warm, open-ended question that invites the participant to share a specific recent experience relevant to your product space. Use the "start with the recent past" technique — ask them to walk you through something they actually did recently, not what they think or prefer. This sets a behavioral tone for the whole conversation.

**Opportunity Exploration**
For each opportunity in the list above, create a clearly labeled sub-section (e.g., "### Exploring: [short label]"). Write 2–3 natural follow-up questions that surface whether this friction or unmet need is real and significant for this customer. Rules:
- Do NOT mention the opportunity statement verbatim
- Do NOT ask leading questions ("Don't you think...") or hypotheticals ("Would you want...")
- DO use "Walk me through the last time you..." or "Tell me about a recent time when..." framing
- DO probe for frequency, workarounds, consequences, and emotional weight
- DO ask what they did next, not what they wish they could do

**Assumption Probes**
If there are assumptions to probe, create a clearly labeled sub-section for each (e.g., "### Probing: [assumption label]"). Write 1–2 indirect behavioral questions to test whether the assumption is grounded in reality. Never ask "Would you pay for X?" or reveal what you are testing. Instead, look for existing behavior that confirms or denies the assumption.

If no assumptions were provided, skip this section.

**Closing**
Write 1–2 closing questions that invite the participant to surface anything important that was not covered, and close warmly.
